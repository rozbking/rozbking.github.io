🌱 The Walstad Method

Natural, Low-Tech, Self-Balancing Ecosystem

The Walstad Method, developed by Diana Walstad (author of "Ecology of the Planted Aquarium"), mimics natural aquatic ecosystems. It uses soil substrate capped with gravel or sand to create a nutrient-rich foundation where plants can thrive without CO2 injection or liquid fertilizers.

Philosophy

"Let nature do the work." In Walstad tanks, plants extract nutrients directly from the soil, fish waste feeds the plants, and beneficial bacteria keep everything balanced. Minimal equipment, minimal intervention, maximum natural beauty.

Why Choose Walstad?

✅ Pros

  • Low maintenance: Infrequent water changes
  • Natural stability: Plants consume ammonia/nitrates, preventing algae blooms (this is best done with larger volumes of water)
  • Educational: A self-contained true ecosystem develops and then, hopefully, balances itself
  • Satisfying: Super cool if pulled off. I personally like a hybrid approach which is Walstad with a bubbler.

⚠️ Cons & Considerations

  • Patience required: Can take 4-6 weeks or much longer to fully cycle and stabilize
  • Cloudy water initially: Soil can leach tannins, making water tea-colored/brown at first
  • Plant selection matters: Need hardy, fast-growing plants to outcompete algae
  • Harder to rescape: Disturbing substrate releases nutrients into water column which can kill your shrimp/snails/fish
  • Not ideal for all fish: Works best with low bioload (shrimp are best)

How to Build a Walstad Tank

1 Choose Your Soil

Best options:

  • Organic potting soil (Miracle-Gro Organic, Black Gold) - NO fertilizers added!
  • Topsoil from garden centers - check ingredients, avoid perlite/vermiculite
  • Sift out large chunks, roots, and bark before using
  • Aim for 1-2 inches depth of soil

2 Cap the Soil

Cover soil with 1-2 inches of:

  • Pool filter sand (looks natural)
  • Black Diamond blasting sand (the popular option)
  • Fine gravel (natural or dark colors work well)

Why cap it? Prevents soil from clouding the water and keeps nutrients locked in substrate.

3 Plant Heavily from Day One

Critical for success! Plants compete with algae for nutrients. Dense planting leads to less algae.

Best Walstad plants:

  • Amazon Sword - Heavy root feeder, loves soil, gets BIG
  • Cryptocoryne (Wendtii, Parva, Spiralis) - Slow but steady, many varieties
  • Java Fern - Attach to driftwood/rocks (with gel superglue), easy to grow
  • Anubias - Incredibly hardy, low light, slow growing
  • Water Sprite - Fast grower, nutrient sponge, floats or planted
  • Hornwort - Algae-fighting, floats or weighted down
  • Dwarf Sagittaria - Carpeting plant that spreads via runners (also good to start glued down)

4 Lighting

Low to moderate light (6-8 hours/day). Too much light can lead to too much algae before plants establish. Start with shorter windows of light exposure (4-6 hours) for first few weeks.

  • LED clip-on lights (Nicrew, Hygger) work great
  • Even basic desk lamps can work if you switch out the bulb for a grow light
  • Avoid intense direct sunlight (can grow algae)- some is fine

5 Filtration (Optional!)

Many Walstad tanks run filterless! Plants do the filtration (this is my version). But gentle flow helps:

  • Sponge filter - Gentle, shrimp-safe, adds surface agitation
  • Small HOB filter - Low flow setting, provides water movement
  • Air stone - Just for gas exchange if no filter - this is my preferred method

6 The Cycling Process

Week 1-2: Water may be cloudy/tea-colored (tannins from soil and wood). This is normal! Do small water changes if too dark.

Week 2-4: Possible algae bloom as nutrients balance in the beginning. Plants are establishing roots.

Week 4-6: Clarity improves, algae decreases, plants start growing. Ecosystem stabilizing!

Week 6+: Once tank is balanced. Add shrimp! 🦐

Pro Tips from Experience

  • Mineralize your soil first (soak, dry, repeat 2-3 times) to reduce initial ammonia spike. I just rinse mine a few times in the sink until the water isn't so dark
  • Add floating plants immediately. This is a game changer as they grow fast and starve algae
  • Don't mess with it too much early on even if that's hard to do. Let the ecosystem and water establish
  • Water changes release nutrients from soil - go easy (10-20% weekly max) - I avoid this altogether
  • Cherry shrimp love a Walstad tank. The biofilm is good shrimp food

Perfect for Shrimp!

Walstad tanks are ideal for shrimp. The soil creates biofilm and microfauna that shrimp graze on constantly. Dense plants, shrimp wood, or dragonstone provide hiding spots for babies. Low flow keeps shrimplets safe. Stable parameters mean happy, multiplying shrimp colonies!

Recommended Stocking for Walstad

  • 5 Shrimp (Neocaridina) per gallon. They will multiply until they stabilize
  • 2-3 Snails - algae cleanup crew. They will also multiply until they stabilize. Some kinds go gang busters (i.e ramshead) so watch out
  • OR 1 small fish (Celestial Pearl Danios, Chili Rasboras) per gallon. Need to get friendly fish to shrimp. Don't recommend unless you have a 5+ gallon tank

Troubleshooting

Cloudy water?

Normal for first 2-4 weeks. Do 20% water changes every 2-3 days if very cloudy. Don't mess with plants/substrate

Green water?

Free-floating algae. Reduce light to 4-6 hours, add more fast-growing plants such as floaters.

Brown algae (diatoms)?

Common in new tanks! Goes away on its own after 2-4 weeks. Snails can help.

Plants melting?

Transition period - some of the stores will grow plants above water. New growth will be aquatic which will lead to die off. Trim dead leaves.
